About This Book

George Orwell's 1984 is the defining dystopian novel of the twentieth century. Set in a totalitarian superstate called Oceania, it follows Winston Smith, a low-ranking Party member who secretly harbors thoughts of rebellion against Big Brother and the all-seeing apparatus of the Party. Written in 1948, its warnings about surveillance, propaganda, and the corruption of truth feel more urgent than ever.

"Who controls the past controls the future. Who controls the present controls the past."

Synopsis

Winston Smith works at the Ministry of Truth, where his job is to rewrite historical records so that the Party's version of events is the only version. London, now the chief city of Airstrip One (formerly Britain), is a decayed, surveilled nightmare where telescreens watch every move and the Thought Police punish even unspoken dissent.

Despite the danger, Winston begins a secret diary — his first act of rebellion. He then begins a forbidden love affair with Julia, a fellow Party member who shares his hatred of the regime. Together they seek out the Brotherhood, a rumored underground resistance led by the enigmatic Emmanuel Goldstein.

But in a world where "Big Brother is watching," freedom is never what it seems. Winston and Julia's rebellion leads them into the hands of O'Brien and the horrors of Room 101, where the Party's true purpose — not merely obedience, but the total destruction of the individual mind — is revealed.

Part One, Chapter 1 — Excerpt

It was a bright cold day in April, and the clocks were striking thirteen. Winston Smith, his chin nuzzled into his breast in an effort to escape the vile wind, slipped quickly through the glass doors of Victory Mansions, though not quickly enough to prevent a swirl of gritty dust from entering along with him.

The hallway smelt of boiled cabbage and old rag mats. At one end of it a coloured poster, too large for indoor display, had been tacked to the wall. It depicted simply an enormous face, more than a metre wide: the face of a man of about forty-five, with a heavy black moustache and ruggedly handsome features.

Key Themes

Totalitarianism: Orwell draws on his observations of both Stalinist Russia and wartime Britain to create a terrifyingly plausible regime. The Party doesn't just demand obedience; it demands love.

Language & Truth: Newspeak — the Party's engineered language — is designed to make dissent literally unthinkable. "Doublethink" allows citizens to hold contradictory beliefs simultaneously. These concepts have entered our everyday vocabulary.

Surveillance & Privacy: The telescreen, the Thought Police, and the omnipresent gaze of Big Brother anticipate modern debates about government surveillance, social media monitoring, and the erosion of privacy.

"War is peace. Freedom is slavery. Ignorance is strength."
