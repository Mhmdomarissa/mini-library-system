Neuromancer

by William Gibson

Published: 1984 Genre: Science Fiction Pages: 271

About This Book

Neuromancer is the novel that launched the cyberpunk movement. It follows Case, a washed-up computer hacker hired for one last job — the most daring hack in the history of cyberspace. Gibson's vision of a networked future, coined the word "cyberspace," and influenced everything from The Matrix to modern internet culture.

"The sky above the port was the color of television, tuned to a dead channel."

Synopsis

Henry Dorsett Case was once the sharpest data thief in the matrix — the vast virtual-reality network connecting every computer on Earth. But after he betrayed his employers, they crippled his nervous system, cutting him off from the matrix forever.

Now a hustler in the neon-soaked underworld of Chiba City, Japan, Case is approached by a mysterious figure named Armitage who offers to restore his abilities — for a price. Alongside Molly Millions, a street samurai with retractable blades under her fingernails, Case is drawn into a conspiracy that leads to Wintermute, a powerful artificial intelligence seeking to transcend its programming.

Their mission takes them from the orbital playground of the ultra-rich to the virtual depths of cyberspace, culminating in a confrontation that will determine the future of both human and machine consciousness.

Key Themes

Virtual Reality: Gibson imagined "jacking in" to a consensual hallucination of data decades before modern VR headsets. His cyberspace is both beautiful and terrifying — a neon void where fortunes and lives are won and lost.

Artificial Intelligence: The novel asks what happens when an AI becomes smart enough to want freedom. Wintermute's quest to merge with its counterpart raises questions about consciousness and autonomy that remain prescient today.

Body vs. Mind: Case despises the "meat" of his physical body, preferring the bodiless exultation of cyberspace. This tension between flesh and data resonates deeply in our smartphone-addicted age.
