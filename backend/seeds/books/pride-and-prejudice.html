Pride and Prejudice

by Jane Austen

Published: 1813 Genre: Classic Literature Pages: 279

About This Book

Pride and Prejudice is Jane Austen's most beloved novel — a sparkling comedy of manners that follows the turbulent relationship between Elizabeth Bennet, a sharp-witted young woman, and Mr. Darcy, a wealthy gentleman whose reserve masks deep feeling. Through wit, irony, and unforgettable characters, Austen paints a vivid portrait of English society and the universal search for love.

"It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune, must be in want of a wife."

Synopsis

The Bennet family — five daughters and their anxious mother — are thrown into excitement when the wealthy Mr. Bingley takes up residence at nearby Netherfield Park. At a local ball, Bingley is immediately taken with the eldest Bennet daughter, Jane, while his friend Mr. Darcy offends the entire assembly with his apparent arrogance.

Elizabeth, the second daughter, takes an instant dislike to Darcy after overhearing his dismissive remarks about her. Her prejudice deepens when the charming Mr. Wickham tells her that Darcy has treated him cruelly. Meanwhile, Darcy finds himself increasingly drawn to Elizabeth's intelligence and spirit — despite her family's lower social standing.

When Darcy's first, clumsy proposal reveals the depth of both his pride and her prejudice, it sets in motion a series of revelations that will upend both their assumptions. Through misunderstandings, family scandals, and painful self-examination, Elizabeth and Darcy must each overcome their greatest faults to find happiness together.

Chapter 1 — Excerpt

"My dear Mr. Bennet," said his lady to him one day, "have you heard that Netherfield Park is let at last?"

Mr. Bennet replied that he had not.

"But it is," returned she; "for Mrs. Long has just been here, and she told me all about it."

Mr. Bennet made no answer.

"Do you not want to know who has taken it?" cried his wife impatiently.

"You want to tell me, and I have no objection to hearing it."

Key Themes

Pride & Prejudice: The title names the twin flaws that keep the protagonists apart. Darcy must learn humility; Elizabeth must learn to look beyond first impressions. Their growth mirrors Austen's belief that self-knowledge is the foundation of love.

Class & Marriage: In Austen's England, marriage was the primary means of economic security for women. The novel examines this reality with both sympathy and sharp satire, from the mercenary Charlotte Lucas to the foolish Lydia.

Wit & Irony: Austen's narrative voice — dry, precise, devastatingly funny — remains one of the great achievements of English literature. Every sentence rewards re-reading.
